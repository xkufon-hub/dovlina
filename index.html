Volume 01 · Beehive anatomy · Four-season observation

The wooden hive is a calendar with wings.

Our first volume looks closely at a colony without turning it into spectacle. The entrance, comb and clustered bees are read as connected parts of a changing seasonal system.

Every photograph is treated as evidence: where bees gather, how wax changes tone and how the boundary between a box and the surrounding landscape remains porous.

01 / SPRING

A colony in motion

A swarm gathered among evergreen branches makes the colony visible as a temporary body. Thousands of bees form a suspended mass while the surrounding bark and needles locate the event outdoors, beyond the geometry of the hive.

02 / SUMMER · ENTRANCE

The busiest threshold

The opening at the base of a wooden box is both runway and boundary. Bees occupy the landing edge and the air above the grass, turning a small architectural detail into the most legible point of traffic.
